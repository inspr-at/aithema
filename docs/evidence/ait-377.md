## Provider-owned integration gaps

1. **S1 and the grant-relative rail timer are opt-in (AIT-383).** `manifest.liveVoice.capabilities.onMicrophoneState` must be `native` or `emulated`. Until it is declared the rail keeps main's Connecting copy, passes the same `deadlineAt` option and adds no connection timer. The real-client controlled-clock regression holds Grok's permission for 60 s with no deadline, while ElevenLabs still reaches its own pre-existing 30 s deadline. With the declaration, `options.onMicrophoneState('requesting' | 'granted')` controls the phase. S1 appears only at `requesting`; credential preparation before that keeps Connecting copy and has a separate 30 s guard starting at `client.start`. `requesting` cancels that guard for the entire visitor decision; `granted` starts a fresh 30 s S3 provider deadline. Typed-only starts use a 30 s deadline from `client.start`. Cleanup and cooldown precede these guards. A capable microphone start receives no pre-grant `deadlineAt`, so providers must honor `signal` and emit `requesting` only for the actual permission wait, then `granted` once acquisition succeeds (already-granted acquisition may report `granted` directly). Deferred-port tests cover a stuck credential request at 30 s, preparation at 20 s followed by a 60 s unbounded prompt, and a fresh grant-relative 30 s, as well as grant-at-20/fail-at-50. No microphone is pre-acquired by the rail.
2. **S5 is off in production until AIT-383.** `manifest.liveVoice.capabilities.replyStarted` must be `native` or `emulated` before any path can arm S5. Connect additionally requires `manifest.liveVoice.capabilities.opener === true`: this boolean promises an unsolicited opener on a fresh connection; absent, `false`, or string values do not qualify. Without an opener, only a user-final event or typed submission arms the 15 s watch. Recovery never arms S5 and clears any previous watch, even when both capabilities are declared. Neither current client declares these signals or the opener flag. Declared clients must supply qualified audible non-filler `speaking` events (`audio: true`, no `filler`); these clear/block S5, including a late audio response. Mode-only speaking does not qualify. Existing non-filler transcript handling and filler exclusion remain. Fake declared clients retain S5 UI/copy/deadline coverage; the real clients prove no watchdog on visitor silence, long speech, silent repair or typed turns. Provider implementation is intentionally untouched.
   **AIT-383 declarations:** ElevenLabs may declare `onMicrophoneState: 'native'`, `replyStarted: 'native'` and `opener: true` only after its adapter supplies the above phase/audio provenance and guarantees its initial spoken opener. Its silent repair stays silent and never arms S5. Grok Voice may declare the two signal capabilities after its adapter implements them, with `opener: false` (or absent): it currently has no unsolicited opener and must wait for a visitor turn. These are future adapter obligations, not declarations added by AIT-377; do not opt either real client in here. If a client's initial connection does not guarantee an opener, it must not declare `true`. AIT-383 must also extend the strict manifest schema and TypeScript declaration (`packages/core/src/plugins.js`, `plugin-contract.d.ts`) with these optional keys, the microphone callback and qualified audio/filler event fields before a real adapter can opt in; today's schema accepts none of the three new keys.

3. **Agent-end charge carry-in is handled conservatively.** The native ElevenLabs `ended` event has no usage. The rail now retains the ended call identity and shows pending charge copy until matching server settlement supplies confirmed usage. If a future event carries a confirmed terminal receipt it can settle immediately. No new client usage event is required to keep today's copy truthful.
